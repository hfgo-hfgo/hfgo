Method Overview

Extrinsic calibration of a multi-camera rig is solved as a sequence of factor graphs rather than one joint problem. Each stage estimates a smaller set of variables against the result of the previous stage, which is held fixed, so the marker map is established before any vehicle camera is introduced.

Stage 1

Environment markers

AprilTag boards observed by the environment camera are merged per board, their relative poses collected as marker-to-marker pairs, and the marker map is solved with Levenberg–Marquardt.

Stage 2

Wheel markers

Wheel-board observations are added on top of the frozen environment map, extending it with the markers that define the vehicle frame.

Stage 3

Vehicle cameras

Each vehicle camera is introduced as a new node against the frozen marker map, and its extrinsics are reported in the vehicle frame.

Observation uncertainty

Every observation carries a full 6×6 pose covariance instead of a hand-tuned scalar weight. Corner-level uncertainty is estimated from the image structure tensor, then propagated through the perspective-n-point solve by an unscented transform. The resulting covariance, off-diagonal terms included, is passed to the solver unchanged, so a marker seen at a shallow angle or far from the image centre contributes proportionally less.

Observation filtering

Outliers are removed one observation at a time rather than by discarding whole images.

  • Marker pairs co-observed fewer times than a threshold are dropped, and only the observations of the less-observed marker in that pair are removed.
  • Vehicle observations that disagree with the frozen marker map beyond a rotation and translation threshold are excluded before the solve.

Both pinhole and fisheye (Kannala–Brandt) camera models are supported, so a rig that mixes wide-angle and telephoto units is handled in a single run.
