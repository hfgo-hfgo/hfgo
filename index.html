Abstract

Fusing the feature, object, and spatial information of multiple cameras with different orientations and fields of view into a common representation such as a bird's-eye view (BEV) requires every camera observation to be aligned to the physical vehicle frame base_link. Under a simple ray model, a 1° yaw mounting error already induces a lateral deviation of about 0.52 m at 30 m ahead. This work addresses offline precise extrinsic calibration performed before driving — after production, maintenance, impact, or sensor replacement — rather than online calibration that compensates for sensor drift while driving.

The core problem in this setting is to produce physical base_link→camera extrinsics for vehicle cameras that cannot observe a common target or scene feature simultaneously, without moving the target vehicle, without camera-pair pre-calibration, and without precision metrology equipment. We propose an uncertainty-aware 2-stage hierarchical factor graph optimization (FGO) framework. An existing corner-to-pose uncertainty propagation yields the full 6×6 covariance of a marker pose from the corner uncertainty of a single image, and its inverse is applied as an observation-wise information matrix to every camera-to-marker (C→M) factor in both stages. Vehicle-independent environment state and vehicle-specific calibration state are separated: Stage 1 builds and freezes an environment marker map from the multi-view observations of a single movable auxiliary camera, and Stage 2 — together with any subsequent calibration in the same physical marker layout — reuses this fixed map to register the vehicle cameras and the wheel markers, producing the physical base_link→camera extrinsics defined by the wheel-marker geometry and the camera-to-camera relative extrinsics derived from them.

In an ablation that keeps the source images, C→M observations, graph, initialization, and optimizer fixed and changes only the information matrix, the proposed weighting attained the lowest rotation, translation, and reprojection RMSE in all nine combinations of three rigs and three noise conditions. In a separate structural comparison under 1 px corner noise, it also attained a lower rotation and translation GT RMSE of the physical base_link-referenced extrinsics than Chain on all three simulation rigs. On eight real-vehicle cameras with overlapping fields of view and no absolute ground truth, the proposed weighting attained the lowest reprojection RMSE and residual standard deviation at each of five stationary poses, as well as the lowest cross-pose median reprojection RMSE. Reusing the frozen map, the Stage 2 optimization time was 61.4% shorter than full_joint on the simulated IONIQ rig and 63.2% shorter on the real IONIQ rig.

Index terms: multi-camera extrinsic calibration, camera-to-vehicle calibration, reusable marker map, hierarchical factor graph optimization, uncertainty-aware marker pose estimation.

Method Overview

The framework is defined by the following sequence. The same C→M observation model is used in both stages, so the graph does not change with the field-of-view overlap of the vehicle cameras.

  1. Observation-wise information matrix. For each image, the camera-to-marker pose is computed with the pinhole or Kannala–Brandt projection model. A single-image corner uncertainty is propagated to a full 6×6 covariance of the marker 6-DoF pose, and the inverse of that covariance is used as the information matrix of the corresponding C→M factor. The unit of the covariance is the marker pose obtained from one image, not a corner and not a pairwise calibration result.
  2. Stage 1 — environment map construction. A single auxiliary camera is moved to several positions and observes the environment markers. One reference marker is fixed as the gauge anchor, and the environment marker poses together with the per-image auxiliary camera poses are optimized into a vehicle-independent environment map.
  3. Map freezing. When the Stage 1 optimization terminates, the estimated environment marker poses are stored as the environment map and frozen.
  4. Stage 2 — fixed-map vehicle calibration. The environment marker poses are no longer estimated. Two connection paths share the one fixed map: the environment-marker observations of each vehicle camera place that camera directly in the common environment, and the path environment map → auxiliary camera → wheel markers places the four wheel markers in the same frame. Vehicle cameras that do not overlap each other are therefore combined in a single environment frame without any camera-pair pre-calibration. Marker-to-marker relative poses are used only for BFS initialization and are never added as optimization factors.
  5. Physical base_link definition. base_link is computed from the four optimized wheel markers. Its axes follow the ISO 8855 convention (+x forward, +y left, +z up) and its origin is the midpoint of the two rear wheel-marker centers.
  6. Extrinsic output. Each vehicle camera pose estimated in the common environment is converted into a base_link→camera extrinsic. The relative extrinsic between any two cameras is obtained by composing the two base_link→camera transforms.

Compared weightings and baselines

  • Proposed — the inverse of the single-image marker pose covariance, applied per observation.
  • Geom-σ — a projection-geometry scalar weight based on projected area, viewing angle, and radial distance from the principal point, following the published formula without any grid search tuned to our results.
  • Fixed-σ — one common information matrix shared by every C→M observation.
  • Chain — a structural baseline that composes the extrinsics along a chain instead of a single joint graph. It is compared on the same stationary data and noise condition.
  • full_joint — a timing baseline that estimates the environment markers, wheel markers, auxiliary cameras, and vehicle cameras in one run with no pre-frozen map.

The three weightings are compared as an ablation: the source images, C→M measurements, observation set, graph structure, initialization, optimizer, and noise realization are all held fixed, and only the factor information matrix changes. Chain and full_joint are not part of that ablation; they are separate structural and timing baselines.

Scope and Limitations

Assumptions

  • Camera intrinsics and projection models are known in advance.
  • Marker IDs, the internal AprilGrid geometry, and the rigidity of each marker board are known.
  • The auxiliary camera can be moved to several positions; the vehicle under calibration stays stationary.
  • Each vehicle camera observes environment markers and can therefore be linked to the frozen map. Direct overlap between vehicle cameras is not required.
  • The physical layout of the environment markers does not change while the frozen map is reused.
  • Wheel markers are rigidly clamped and are re-observed and re-estimated for every vehicle.

What is not claimed

  • No novelty is claimed for data-driven information matrices in general, for 6×6 pose covariance estimation itself, for marker-infrastructure reuse, or for unified graphs that remove pairwise pre-calibration.
  • No accuracy equivalence between the frozen-map hierarchical run and full_joint is claimed.
  • No standard-benchmark SOTA ranking is claimed. The nuPlan and PAI camera configurations are reproduced in simulation, and the original datasets are not used as benchmarks.
  • No statistical significance is claimed, and no online extrinsic update, frame rate, or real-time inference is claimed.

Known limitations

  • The real session has no absolute ground truth for the environment markers or the vehicle extrinsics.
  • The 8 evaluated real cameras have overlapping fields of view, so the real data provides no non-overlapping evidence.
  • An error in the environment map propagates in common to every subsequent vehicle that shares that map.
  • Marker printing tolerance, board flatness, intrinsic residuals, and wheel-clamp mounting error may not be fully represented by single-image corner randomness alone.
  • The double-solution ambiguity of planar markers can affect candidate selection, refinement, and failure handling.
  • Some equidistant cameras in the IONIQ simulation reproduce the horizontal field of view of real wide-angle pinhole cameras, but the projection geometry differs, so those results are not transferred to real wide-angle pinhole performance.
  • Joint calibration of intrinsics, time synchronization, rolling shutter, and LiDAR extrinsics is out of scope, as is detecting or updating a changed environment map.
